# Multistage, Self‐Sensing Artificial Muscles With Coordination From Liquid Crystal Elastomer‐Graphene Composite Fibers

## 摘要与研究价值

**原文摘要:** ABSTRACT Skeletal muscles produce coordinated large, smooth, reversible, and precise deformations through integrated sensing, actuation, and feedback control. Replicating this level of multifunctionality in artificial muscles remains elusive, particularly because many soft actuators lack real‐time, on‐demand control over intermediate actuation states. Here, we introduce CoilLCE, an electronically controlled artificial muscle comprising liquid crystal elastomer (LCE)/graphene composite fibers embedded with a flexible, self‐sensing Joule‐heating copper coil. By exploiting the well‐defined monotonic relationship between temperature and actuation strain, CoilLCE enables closed‐loop, multistage actuation with programmable intermediate states. We further reveal a muscle‐like soft yet tough mechanical response in LCE/graphene composites with only 0.25 wt.% graphene. The reduced modulus facilitates antagonistic coordination, achieving an antagonistic actuation strain of 16.1 ± 0.7%, compared with 11.2 ± 0.8% for pure LCE, corresponding ∼44% enhancement, while preserving high actuation performance, yielding a large actuation strain (> 40%), strain rates up to 30% s −1 , and a work capacity of 343 J kg −1 . As demonstrations, CoilLCE‐actuated robotic fingers play piano keys with tunable force and speed, and four antagonistic CoilLCE pairs generate on‐demand eye movements. This work advances LCEs from programmable shape‐morphing materials toward controllable, self‐sensing, and system‐integrable artificial muscles for dexterous, adaptive, and expressive soft robotics.
