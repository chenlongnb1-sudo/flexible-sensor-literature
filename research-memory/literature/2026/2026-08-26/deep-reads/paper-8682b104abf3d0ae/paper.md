# Ballistic‐Like Thermal Transport Between Fillers in Highly Conductive Stretchable Nanocomposites

- 期刊：Advanced Functional Materials
- 日期：2026-08-24
- DOI：10.1002/adfm.77937
- 解析状态：metadata_only_no_legal_pdf

## 摘要与研究价值

**Original:** ABSTRACT Heat transport across a potential energy barrier is largely unexplored in bulk nanocomposites. Here we report ballistic‐like thermal transport between fillers in conductive nanocomposites, where thermal conductivity ( κ ) increases as channel length (i.e., barrier width, δ B ) is increased by stretching, maintaining strain‐invariant thermal conductance. This is enabled by in situ generated silver nanosatellite (AgNS) particles (3.4 nm), realizing a short δ B (4.1 nm). The barrier height ( λ B ) is also tuned to be negligible by crosslinking silicone rubber matrix. The κ (21.94 Wm − 1 K − 1 ) is also considerably greater than the uncrosslinked nanocomposite without AgNS particles. The stretching does not introduce additional scattering between AgNS particles, since δ B is smaller than phonon mean free path (9.3 nm), although scattering exists in the bulk nanocomposite. The transport changes depending on δ B and λ B , resulting in diffusive transport ( δ B ≥ 1.3 µm, regardless of λ B ) and tunneling‐dominated ( δ B λ B ) regimes. The ballistic‐like thermal transport can be utilized for flexible electronics and biomedical applications where strain‐invariant heat dissipation is required.
