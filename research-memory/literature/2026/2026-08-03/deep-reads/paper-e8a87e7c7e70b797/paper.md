## 摘要与研究价值

**Original:** ABSTRACT A loudspeaker is an essential component in interactive electronics; however, conventional sound devices rely on electromechanical systems with inherently rigid moving parts, limiting their integration into emerging soft electronics. This work demonstrates a soft and stretchable thermoacoustic (TA) loudspeaker utilizing geometrically aligned carbon nanotubes (CNTs) partially embedded in an elastomeric substrate. When an electrical sound input is applied, the geometrically unique features of the aligned CNTs, which have a large surface‐to‐volume ratio with a high aspect ratio, enable Joule heating to effectively excite the surrounding air, generating audible sound without moving parts. These geometrical features remain stable even under 100% tensile strain, with only a 1.81 dB variation in sound pressure level (SPL).
