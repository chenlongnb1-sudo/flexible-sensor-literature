## 摘要与研究价值

**Original:** ABSTRACT Active on/off valves serve as critical components for rapid flow switching in fluid systems, playing a vital role in industrial machinery, biochemical analysis, and soft robotics. As a pivotal performance metric, driving forces directly govern switching dynamics and regulation capacity of the overall system. However, existing reviews are typically confined to a single driving‐force magnitude, leaving a gap in systematic summaries that span the full range from millinewtons (mN) to kilonewtons (kN). To bridge this gap, this review provides the first comprehensive synthesis of active on/off valve technologies across the entire driving force range. This paper delineates two distinct technological trajectories: high‐driving‐force valves (N–kN) are evolving toward high‐frequency response with precision control of large flow rates, while low‐driving‐force valves (mN–N) are transitioning from basic flow regulation toward multifunctional integration, embedding self‐sensing, self‐healing, and self‐powering capabilities. This review delves into the latest developments in actuation mechanisms, structural designs, substrate materials, and working media. Furthermore, the discussion extends to modeling methodologies, control strategies, and practical applications across various domains such as digital hydraulics, microfluidics, and soft robotics. Finally, this review identifies persistent challenges and proposes forward‐looking perspectives to guide the development of next‐generation high‐performance active on/off valves.
